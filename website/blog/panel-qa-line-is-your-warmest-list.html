Conference tactics

The panel Q&A line is your warmest contact list at any conference

2026-08-22 · 6 min read

You walked into the sequencer panel at 2pm. The floor opens at 2:45. Eight hands go up. Each person just told you, on a public stage, exactly what they care about — and almost every attendee will walk past that line on the way to the next booth.

By the time you get to the afterparty, you remember the panelists. You do not remember the line.

The self-qualifying queue

Most networking is a filter problem. You meet 200 people, you need 6.

The Q&A line is the only place at a crypto conference where the filter runs itself. Someone who raises their hand has self-identified as invested in the topic, articulate enough to formulate a question in public, and willing to spend social capital on the answer. CEIR's exhibitor research puts face-to-face event interactions as the highest-converting B2B channel — and Forrester's event data shows panels and roundtables outperform booth traffic on lead quality.

The Q&A line is a pre-qualified pipeline. You didn't find them — they announced themselves.

Why the line beats the afterparty

The afterparty has volume. The line has signal.

At a 1,000-person afterparty, you might shake 40 hands. Of those, maybe 4 are pre-qualified. The other 36 are noise. At a panel Q&A, the people who asked questions have already filtered themselves. They sat through the talk, formulated a sharp question, and raised their hand in front of 400 people. That is a stronger signal than a handshake in a loud room.

  • The Q&A person has a specific problem the panel addressed — they have intent
  • They spoke publicly — you have a topic anchor for the next message
  • They're still standing in line when the panel ends — no cold-open required
  • They're alone with their question — no group dynamic to navigate

Forrester's B2B event data shows that while 80-90% of event leads are never followed up with, the 10% that do convert at rates 3-5x higher than cold outreach. The Q&A line is where that 10% lives.

The capture window

The Q&A line evaporates in 30 minutes. The panelists are ushered to a green room and the question-askers are dispersing into the corridor. Pick one window before the panel starts:

  • During the Q&A — sit near the mic. Write down one phrase per question: their handle, their company, the exact words they used. Most conferences have 5-8 questions; capture all of them.
  • Immediately after — walk to the line within 90 seconds of the panel ending. "Hey, I liked your question about X — what are you working on?" beats every afterparty script.
  • At the speaker meet-and-greet — some conferences route Q&A askers to a side room with the panelists. Warmer still.

The worst window is "later." The line is gone.

What to do with the list

The Q&A list is a precision play: 8 messages, high reply rate, sent within 24 hours. The afterparty list is volume: 200 messages, low reply rate, filtered later.

The follow-up has a built-in opener — the exact question they asked. "You asked about L2 sequencer decentralisation at the 2pm panel. Would a 15-minute call be useful?" They will remember the question. They will remember you asked.

The math: spend 80% of your follow-up effort on 4% of your contacts. The 192 afterparty handshakes get a generic template. The 8 Q&A askers get a tailored message each.
