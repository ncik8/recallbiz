The 90-minute morning-after protocol after a crypto event

2026-07-30 · 4 min read

By the time you wake up the morning after Token2049, your phone has 200 Telegram QRs, 14 paper cards in your jacket, and a voice memo titled "party hotel 1am" that you do not remember recording. The closing party is over. The reply window is not.

The original InsideSales.com lead-response study, replicated by dozens of sales teams since 2011, found that a lead contacted within 5 minutes is 21 times more likely to qualify than one contacted after 30 minutes. After 24 hours, the conversion curve flattens almost to zero. For a 200-person conference, that math collapses into a single morning: if you do not triage and send within 90 minutes of waking up, you have lost half the value of the room.

The five-step morning ritual

The protocol is small on purpose. It runs ninety minutes from coffee to closed laptop. The point is not to write the perfect follow-up to everyone. The point is to rank 200 contacts into three buckets before the day's distractions arrive.

The reply curve is not a slope. It is a cliff at five minutes, a slope for the next hour, and a flatline by Tuesday. Your morning decides which one you land on.

The five steps, in order:

  • Forward everything (5 min). Every QR, every paper card photo, every voice memo from last night. Get it out of Telegram DMs and into one place before you start judging it.
  • Tag by context (10 min). One tag per contact: which panel, which afterparty, which booth, which coffee queue. Granularity beats volume here. "DePIN panel, second row" beats "conference."
  • Rank into three buckets (15 min). A-tier (people you genuinely need to talk to this week), B-tier (interesting, this month), C-tier (cold). Most of your 200 will be C. That is fine.
  • Draft A-tier follow-ups first (30 min). Three sentences each, one specific callback to the conversation, one clear next step. Not "great meeting you."
  • Schedule B-tier for day 7, 30, 90 (15 min). Anything that needs warming lives in the queue, not in your morning. The protocol is about ruthless prioritisation, not volume.

Why the morning, specifically

HubSpot's recurring event-marketing analyses keep landing on the same finding: contacts followed up within 24 hours convert at roughly three times the rate of contacts followed up within a week, and the gap between hour-one and hour-twenty-four is steeper than most people realise. The catch is that "within 24 hours" almost always means "in the morning after." By lunch you have meetings. By 3pm you have email. By 5pm you have the next event on your calendar.

There is a second reason. The 90-minute window is short enough to defend. A four-hour "post-event processing block" gets cancelled by tiredness, jet lag, and the seductive decision to start tomorrow instead. A ninety-minute ritual with coffee at the centre is something you can do on a Singapore layover or a Dubai hotel room.

The taxonomy is the product

The actual deliverable from the morning is not a list of follow-up emails. It is a ranked pipeline. After the protocol, you should be able to answer four questions in under ten seconds:

  • Who are the seven people I owe a message before noon?
  • Which contacts came from a context I care about (the panel, the afterparty, the booth)?
  • What is the open loop I promised to close for each A-tier contact?
  • Which B-tier contacts deserve a calendar slot in week two, not week twelve?

If you cannot answer those, the 90 minutes did not happen. Forwarding 200 QRs into a database is not work. Ranking them into a pipeline is.

The morning-after is also where most contact systems quietly fail. People capture beautifully during the event. Then they sleep. Then they fly home. Then they triage "next weekend." By Monday, the curation tax has compounded: every hour of delay makes each follow-up feel heavier, which makes the next one less likely to ship. The protocol exists to remove the choice. Coffee, 90 minutes, ranked pipeline, done.
