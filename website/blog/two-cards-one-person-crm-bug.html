Two cards for one person is the most expensive CRM bug

2026-08-26 · 5 min read

You shook a hand at Token2049 on Day 1. You met them again at the afterparty on Day 2. By the time you land home, your contact list has the same person saved twice — and the second card has slightly different data than the first.

That's not unusual. It's the default. And it's the most expensive bug in any networking system, because it costs you in trust instead of storage.

The official duplicate rate

Salesforce's own 2024 research on data validation found that more than 25% of customer records in the average B2B database contain duplicates. HubSpot, MarketingSherpa, and SuperAGI converge on a similar number: roughly 22.5% to 30% of B2B data goes bad every year through job changes, handle updates, profile pic swaps, and plain human error.

Salesforce's number is the floor. At a multi-day crypto event, the rate runs higher. Here's why.

You don't have a contact list. You have a contact list and the same person three times under three slightly different spellings.

Why conferences triple the rate

The duplicate rate climbs every time someone touches your contact list. Telegram handles change. Profile pictures update. Names get misspelled on the second card because the second introduction happened in a louder room. People RSVP under one handle to a side event, then show up under a different username to the afterparty. AI vision OCRs a business card at an oblique angle and you save "Sasha Linch" instead of "Sasha Lynch."

Conference attendees are also the population most likely to change roles. Industry analyses cited in HubSpot research note tech contact lists decay at around 40% annually — and the people you meet at a crypto conference are disproportionately founders, BD leads, and crypto-native operators who change titles every 9 to 14 months.

You don't meet the average professional at a crypto event. You meet the people whose job titles are about to change.

The follow-up is where the bug costs you

Most people think "duplicate" means wasted storage. The real cost is the follow-up.

When you have two cards for the same person, three bad things happen in the first week after the event:

  • You draft the same follow-up message twice and send it twice — once from each card — which immediately flags you as automated.
  • You meet the person again on Telegram and accidentally message them under the wrong saved name. They know you forgot. You know you forgot.
  • You merge the two cards weeks later and lose the richer note attached to the second one — usually the one with the actual conversation anchor.

The duplicate is the visible scar. The lost anchor is what you actually lose.

The fix: capture-time dedupe, not quarterly cleanup

You can't dedupe a 200-card list by hand at 1am after the afterparty. You can't dedupe it at all when you're flying home the next morning. Quarterly cleanups miss it because the damage is done in the first 48 hours.

The system that works is capture-time. Forward the QR. Forward the same QR again when you meet them a second time. The system should look at the underlying identity — the Telegram handle, the email, the phone — and tell you you already saved this person before you add a second card.

That's the entire job: one identity per person, every card attached to the right anchor, merged the moment the second capture happens — not six months later when you've already lost the trust.
