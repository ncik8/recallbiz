Conference tactics

You collected 200 contacts at Token2049. You actually needed 6.

trce · 2026-08-21 · 5 min read

By the time you walked out of Token2049 Marina Bay, your Telegram had 200 new forwards sitting in @trceiobot. By the cab to Changi, you felt like you'd won. By Monday morning, you'd opened 12 of them. By Friday, you'd replied to 6. That's your entire conference ROI in a single number.

The myth of high-volume networking is that more captures equal more deals. The math says the opposite. Of the 200 contacts you collected, only ~6 will produce a real reply, a real meeting, or a real check. The other 194 are noise — not because they're bad people, but because they weren't the right 6.

The Pareto math nobody runs

Bain & Company's classic NPS research shows 80% of a company's revenue comes from 20% of its customers. The same ratio lives inside a conference contact list, only it's closer to 97/3 than 80/20. InsideSales' 2024 sales benchmark reports that the median B2B reply rate on cold outreach hovers around 1-3%; for warm conference intros, it climbs to 8-12%, but only when the message lands within the first 24 hours. After that, you're back in the cold pile.

Run the numbers on 200 captures, an 8% reply rate, and a 30% meeting-to-opportunity conversion: 200 × 0.08 × 0.30 = 4.8. The honest ceiling on a 200-contact event is five real opportunities. If you're unusually good at follow-up cadence and have a strong offer, you might hit eight. You are not going to hit forty.

The goal at a 200-person event isn't to capture 200 contacts. It's to identify the six who matter before the week ends — and let the other 194 sit, unfollowed, unbothered, unarchived.

The selection happens before the cab ride

By the time you're on the way home, the post-event decay has already started. CEIR's exhibitor research finds that companies who triage contacts within 24 hours of an event report 4-5x higher ROI than those who wait a week. The triage is the work. The capture was the easy part.

Here's the triage system that turns 200 captures into 6 real follow-ups:

  • Tag within 24 hours. Open @trceiobot the morning after. For each contact, type a single word: hot, warm, or archive. Hot means you'd take a meeting this week. Warm means maybe in 90 days. Archive means no follow-up — and that's fine.
  • Hot gets a one-line personal note. Not a template. One sentence that references the specific thing you talked about at the booth or afterparty. This is the only thing that survives the inbox.
  • Warm gets a sequence, not a message. Three touches across 30 days, each referencing a different angle of the conversation you had. Reply.ai's 2024 multi-touch data shows 3-touch sequences lift reply rates by 47% over single sends.
  • Archive gets nothing. The discipline is in the silence. Sales rep ramp studies consistently show that "untrained follow-up" of lukewarm contacts tanks sender reputation faster than no follow-up at all.

Why the 194 don't matter (and never did)

There's a guilt that lives inside every high-volume networker: the unspoken belief that if you just sent the right email to the right person from the side-event bathroom line, that deal would close. It wouldn't. Outbound sales teams have known this for a decade: the long tail of contacts costs more in time than it ever returns in pipeline.

The job isn't to maximize the number of people you email. The job is to find the six who'd reply to a message that mentioned a real conversation you actually had. Those six exist inside the 200. They almost never exist inside the random sample of 60 you'd email if you tried to email everyone.

The 90-minute Monday protocol

Block 90 minutes on the Monday after the event. Forward every saved contact to @trceiobot and tag them with the hot, warm, or archive keyword. Send the hot messages by hand. Schedule the warm sequences. Let the archive tag protect your reputation.

Six weeks later, you'll have replied to six real conversations, met three of them for coffee, and closed at least one deal that paid for the flight. The other 194 contacts will still be in your database, searchable in 4 seconds when the moment is right — and forgotten when it isn't.

That's the system. Capture everything. Follow up with almost no one. Treat the long tail as a future-search index, not a current obligation.
