Follow-up systems

The conference triage: who deserves your next message

2026-07-25 · 5 min read

You walked into the hotel lobby after a two-day summit with 86 new contacts and one flight home. By the time your tray table folds down, every name looks equally urgent, so you either message everyone badly or nobody at all.

The list is not the problem. The mistake is treating every handshake as the same stage of a relationship. A useful post-event system does not ask, “Who did I meet?” It asks, “Who has a live reason to hear from me next?”

A contact list is not a queue

Bain & Company’s customer-loyalty research has a useful warning for conference networking: the most profitable 20% of customers typically generate more than 100% of a company’s profit. That is not a license to dismiss the other 80%. It is a reminder that attention is a resource, and a flat list hides where it matters most.

Your contact database also gets less reliable with age. Nucleus Research estimates that 25–30% of CRM contact data decays every year as people change roles, companies, and numbers. A contact with no next action does not stay neutral; it becomes harder to recognise and easier to ignore.

The goal after an event is not to follow up with everyone. It is to identify who has a live reason to hear from you next.

Use three buckets, not a lead score

Skip the elaborate scorecard. Give every new contact one temporary label, based on the next useful action:

  • Now: there is a clear promise, problem, or introduction to continue this week.
  • Soon: the fit is real and the conversation had signal, but no immediate action exists.
  • Archive: the exchange was pleasant, but you cannot name a reason to reopen it yet.

These are not judgments about people. They are instructions for your future self. “Now” needs a specific message. “Soon” needs a reminder and a searchable context note. “Archive” needs permission to wait instead of a guilt-driven follow-up that says nothing.

Triage in ten minutes

Run three yes-or-no questions while the event is still in your head: Did they name a problem you can help with? Did either of you commit to an object, introduction, or next step? Would a message this week feel like a continuation rather than a cold restart? Two yes answers usually means Now; one means Soon; zero means Archive.

Do not move the decision into a separate spreadsheet. Salesforce’s 2026 State of Sales report says 51% of sales leaders identify tech silos as a barrier to their efforts. If the label lives away from the conversation, the label becomes another piece of work. Add it beside the contact, using plain terms such as now, soon, or archive. Capture the label while saving the contact: forward it to @trceiobot, add the event and one sentence about the signal, then return to the room.

Try it

On your next flight home, do not start by drafting 86 messages. Search the event, assign each contact to Now, Soon, or Archive, and write only the first sentence for the Now group. A Now message continues the exact thread; a Soon message preserves the signal without pretending there is an urgent deal; Archive waits for a real reason. You will finish with fewer messages, better context, and a clear reason for every follow-up you send.
