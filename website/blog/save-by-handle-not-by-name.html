Telegram-native workflows

Stop saving by name. Save contacts by Telegram handle.

trce · 2026-09-01 · 5 min read

By the time you left Token2049, you'd met seven Vitaliks. Three weeks later, you search your contact list for "Vitalik" and get eight people, half of whom you cannot remember meeting.

The problem is not your memory. The problem is the unit. A name is a string with millions of collisions. A Telegram handle is a globally unique identifier that resolves to exactly one inbox. It is the only contact field from a crypto event that still works six months later.

The five things in a Telegram identity

Most people only know about one of them:

  • Display name — cosmetic label. Changes weekly.
  • @username — globally unique public handle. The thing people actually search for.
  • Numeric user ID — the permanent internal anchor. Never changes, even across rebrand.
  • Phone number — gated by privacy settings. Rarely the right capture target.
  • Bio + profile photo — the context layer that turns a handle into a person.

The single best thing you can capture from a conference handshake is the @username. Not the phone, not a LinkedIn URL, not an email. The handle is the only field that opens a DM in one tap, and Telegram DM open rates sit above 90% — compared to 20-30% for cold email and 57.5% for LinkedIn InMail.

Why the handle beats the name

The handle survives every other piece of contact data dying around it. Title changes. Company pivots. Reorgs. Rebrands. A good Telegram handle is the part of a person that stays.

Names decay in three predictable ways:

  • Ambiguity. "Daniel Park" returns forty results. "@danielpark_eth" returns one.
  • Volatility. People change titles, companies, and display names on a whim. Their handle stays.
  • Action distance. To email someone you need their email, an open inbox, and a non-spammy subject line. To DM a handle: type, send. Five seconds total.

LinkedIn has spent a decade trying to make InMail work. The data is telling: 57.5% open rate sounds great until you see response rates of 4.77% in software and 10.42% in legal services, per Overloop's 2026 benchmarks. The handle-to-DM path skips the entire waiting-for-acceptance step.

What to save, in order

At the moment of introduction, you have about ten seconds. Capture in this order, and only this order:

  1. The @username — typed or screenshotted. This is the contact.
  2. One anchor sentence — "thinks ZK rollups are overhyped" beats "works on ZK" by a factor of ten in recall.
  3. The context tag — which panel, which afterparty, which table. The scene, not the company.

Skip the rest. The LinkedIn URL, the website, the long-form bio — all of that can be reconstructed from the handle, because every Telegram profile carries the bio and link fields right under the username.

What changes when your list is handle-first

Six weeks after an event, three things become possible that were not possible with a name-based list:

  • You can find them. Search by handle fragment, by what they said, by who introduced you.
  • You can message them. No connection request, no InMail credit, no connect button. One DM, one tap.
  • You can stitch their identity. When they rebrand or pivot, the handle ties the new identity back to the person you met.

The handle is also why Telegram groups are quietly the most valuable contact surface in crypto. Everyone in the 800-person Token2049 group has a public handle — and the handle is what makes the group searchable six months later, after the group has gone quiet.
