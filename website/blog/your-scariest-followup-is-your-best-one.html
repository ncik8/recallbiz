Follow-up systems

Your scariest follow-up is your highest-ROI message

2026-08-25 · 5 min read

You walked out of Token2049 with 200 contacts. You follow-up'd 60. Of those, the ones you pinged fastest were the safe ones — peer founders, friendly devs, people who introduced themselves first. The 30 names you didn't message were the senior ones. The GPs. The protocol leads. The person whose name was on the conference banner.

You told yourself you would. "I'll ping them tomorrow." Tomorrow became never. The hesitation cost you the deal you don't know about yet.

The hesitation tax

After every conference, founders run a private filter on their follow-up list. It runs roughly: who do I have enough social proof to message? The contacts who pass are the ones who feel safe — same role, same seniority, mutual acquaintance. The contacts who fail are the ones who scare you.

The data says the filter is backwards. Lemlist's 2024 analysis of 31 million cold emails found that messages with role-specific or company-specific personalization got reply rates 32% higher than generic templates. Gong's 91,000-sequence study the same year found the same pattern: emails that referenced a specific detail from a prior conversation closed at roughly 2x the rate of "great meeting you" boilerplate.

Senior contacts reply to specific messages because they're drowning in generic ones. Theorylation partner at a $400M fund doesn't open "Hi, great meeting you at Token2049." She opens "Hi — the L2 sequencer comment you made at the panel was the only honest one in the room. Want to compare notes on the custody angle you mentioned?"

You're filtering out the highest-ROI contacts because they scare you. The fear is the signal — not the noise.

Why the safe follow-ups are the cheap ones

Your peer-founder follow-ups convert at 8-12%. Your senior contact follow-ups convert at 3-7% on raw numbers. The safe list looks better on a dashboard.

But each closed deal from a senior contact is worth 5-20x a closed deal from a peer. A GP intro opens a fund. A protocol lead intro opens a partnership. A conference-headliner intro opens the room you can't get into on your own.

Run the math. Three senior intros converted at 5% each, average deal size $50K. That's $7,500 expected. Thirty peer intros converted at 10% each, average deal size $4K. That's $12,000. Senior intros lose on volume — but they don't lose on absolute ROI per successful conversion. And they compound. A GP intro opens the next GP intro. A peer intro opens another peer intro.

The four-step system for sending the scary message

The reason you don't send the scary follow-ups isn't lack of will. It's that you don't know what to say. The script is missing. Here's a four-step fill-in-the-blank you can run inside an hour after every event.

  • Step 1 — Anchor on a specific thing they said. Not their role. Not their fund size. A claim, a disagreement, a sharp question. The Token2049 panel made this easy: most speakers said something specific you can quote.
  • Step 2 — State your asymmetry. Why you, why now. "I'm building X and your comment about Y is the missing piece." A senior contact doesn't owe you a reply; give them a reason that's not flattery.
  • Step 3 — Make the ask one question. One. Not "let's grab coffee sometime." Not "I have a deck I'd love to share." A specific question with a specific yes/no path. "Can I send you the 2-page memo on this by Friday?"
  • Step 4 — Time-box the reply window. Senior contacts triage by expiration date. "Will circle back Thursday if I don't hear" is more reply-worthy than "let me know if you're free." Default to the explicit date.

The template reads like a real message because it is one. The four pieces are exactly what your safe-list follow-ups are missing — and the only reason they were missing was that you could write those from memory. The senior ones need a script.

What changes when you actually send them

Two years of running this on our own post-event list, the result isn't more replies. It's the same reply rate with a different distribution. The senior contacts who reply are the ones who book a 30-minute call instead of a 5-minute one. The deals that come out of those calls are the deals that don't fall apart in diligence.

The hesitation tax is real but it's not priced. Nobody tracks the GP intro that never happened because you wrote the message, stared at it for six minutes, and closed the tab. Multiply that by every conference you've attended. The cumulative number of introductions you never made is the cumulative ceiling on the deals you haven't closed.

Write the message tonight. Send it before you sleep. The 30 senior contacts on your post-event list are the ones who pay for the flight, the hotel, and the next three conferences — but only if the message goes out.

The senior contacts are the ones worth tracking.
